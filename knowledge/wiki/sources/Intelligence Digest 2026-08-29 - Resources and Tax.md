## Fuel tax credits - the debate is intensifying

- The diesel rebate is a growing 2026 pressure point; mining receives about
  50% of total credit payments vs ~15% for agriculture. Sources: Hancock
  Prospecting commentary (https://www.hancockprospecting.com.au/diesel-rebate-debate-set-to-intensify-in-2026-ag-sector-warned/),
  Australia Institute (https://australiainstitute.org.au/post/scrap-fuel-tax-rebates-for-mining-industry-not-farmers/). [unreviewed]
- Leading reform proposal: a $50M annual cap on the ~15-18 largest claimants,
  overflow to a decarbonisation fund; farmers/truckers/small business
  untouched. Fortescue is campaigning FOR the cap - a split inside mining.
  Sources: https://discoveryalert.com.au/fuel-tax-credit-cap-mining-diesel-subsidy-australia/ ,
  https://www.fortescue.com/en/real-zero/diesel-fuel-rebate . [unreviewed]
- MCA's public line: the campaign against the credit is "an attack on regional
  Australia and jobs"; the credit is a refund of a road tax on fuel never used
  on roads. Source: https://minerals.org.au/resources/campaign-to-scrap-fuel-tax-credit-an-attack-on-regional-australia-and-jobs/ [client position - accepted as their stated stance]
- NFF: removal would raise food production costs and damage regional
  economies. [unreviewed]
- Decision points to watch: Labor national conference (July 2026, passed -
  check outcomes) and the mid-year budget update late 2026.
- Opposition landscape: Climate Council frames the rebate as "paying big
  miners to pollute" (https://www.climatecouncil.org.au/what-is-the-diesel-fuel-rebate-and-why-is-the-government-paying-big-miners-to-pollute/). [opposition frame]

## Critical minerals - tailwind for the sector

- $1.2B Critical Minerals Strategic Reserve targeting antimony, gallium and
  rare earths; production-incentive consultation closed 11 Aug 2026.
  Sources: https://www.industry.gov.au/mining-oil-and-gas/minerals/critical-minerals ,
  https://discoveryalert.com.au/strategic-resource-independence-australia-critical-minerals-2026/ [unreviewed]
- US-Australia Critical Minerals Framework Agreement analysed by CSIS; Japan
  partnership also active - security framing available to the industry.
  Source: https://www.csis.org/analysis/unpacking-us-australia-critical-minerals-framework-agreement [unreviewed]
